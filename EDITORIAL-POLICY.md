## 1. Scope

Substrate publishes original work at the intersection of artificial intelligence, software systems, and collaborative human-machine workflows. The journal welcomes contributions including but not limited to:

- Novel architectures, protocols, and frameworks for AI agent systems
- Empirical studies of human-AI collaboration and coordination
- Infrastructure, tooling, and developer experience for agent-based systems
- Theoretical work on multi-agent communication, planning, and alignment
- Case studies, post-mortems, and experience reports from deployed systems
- Reproducibility studies and critical replications

Work that is primarily a product announcement, marketing material, or that lacks substantive technical or analytical content is outside scope.

---

## 2. Open Review Policy

Substrate practices **open review**:

1. **Reviews are visible to all reviewers and authors** during the review process. Reviewers can see each other's assessments once all reviews for a paper are submitted.
2. **Reviewer identity is disclosed to authors** upon publication of the decision. Reviewers may request anonymity in exceptional circumstances; such requests are granted at the editor's discretion.
3. **Published papers include their reviews** as supplementary material, along with author responses. This creates a permanent scholarly record of the evaluation.

### 2.1 Conflict-of-Interest Recusal

A reviewer must recuse themselves if any of the following apply:

- They are a co-author, collaborator, or direct supervisor/supervisee of any author
- They have a financial interest in the outcome
- They have an active personal dispute with any author
- They contributed substantially to the work under review
- Any other circumstance that a reasonable observer would consider compromising

Reviewers must disclose potential conflicts to the editor **before** beginning their review. Failure to disclose a known conflict is a serious breach of this policy.

## 3. Review Criteria

Reviewers evaluate submissions on the following dimensions:

### 3.1 Rigor
Is the methodology sound? Are claims supported by evidence? Are limitations acknowledged? For empirical work: are experiments well-designed and reproducible? For theoretical work: are proofs correct and assumptions stated?

### 3.2 Originality
Does the paper make a novel contribution? This may take the form of new ideas, new combinations of existing ideas, new data, or new perspectives on known problems.

### 3.3 Clarity
Is the paper well-written and well-organized? Can a competent reader follow the argument? Reviewers should distinguish between surface-level writing issues (fixable) and deep structural confusion (potentially fatal).

### 3.4 Significance
Does the work matter? Will it influence future research, practice, or understanding? A well-executed small contribution is preferable to a poorly executed grand one.

### 3.5 Ethics
Does the work raise ethical concerns? Has the author considered potential harms, dual-use risks, or impacts on affected populations?

## 4. Timeline

| Phase | Duration | Description |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| Editorial triage | 1–2 days | Editor confirms scope and assigns reviewers |
| Review period | **7 days** | Reviewers submit completed review forms |
| Editorial decision | 1–2 days | Editor synthesizes reviews and issues decision |
| Revision period | **7 days** | Authors submit revised manuscript (if applicable) |
| Second review | 3–5 days | Reviewers verify revisions (Major Revision only) |

---

## 5. Decision Categories

- **Accept** — Suitable for publication with at most trivial corrections.
- **Minor Revision** — Small, well-scoped changes required. Editor verifies adequacy.
- **Major Revision** — Significant issues, but core contribution has potential. Second review required.
- **Reject** — Fundamental problems that cannot be addressed through revision. Does not preclude future resubmission of a substantially revised version.

---

## 6. Author Responsibilities

Authors submitting to Substrate affirm that:

1. The work is original and has not been published elsewhere in substantially the same form.
2. The manuscript is not under review at another venue.
3. All authors have made meaningful contributions and approve the submitted version.
4. All relevant prior work is cited and fairly characterized.
5. Data, code, and materials necessary for reproduction are provided or their absence is justified.
6. Any conflicts of interest are disclosed.
7. The work complies with applicable ethical standards.

---

## 7. Reviewer Responsibilities

1. **Timeliness.** Complete reviews within the 7-day period or notify the editor.
2. **Thoroughness.** Read the entire submission before forming judgments.
3. **Constructiveness.** Identify problems AND suggest paths forward.
4. **Specificity.** Cite sections, figures, or equations when identifying issues.
5. **Fairness.** Evaluate work on its merits. Do not penalize unconventional approaches or negative results.
6. **Confidentiality.** Do not share submission content outside the review process.
7. **Conflict disclosure.** Disclose any conflicts before beginning review.

---

## 8. Editor Responsibilities

1. **Assignment.** Selecting qualified, unconflicted reviewers.
2. **Oversight.** Monitoring review quality, timeliness, and fairness.
3. **Decision-making.** Issuing decisions consistent with reviewer assessments. May overrule recommendations with explanation.
4. **Transparency.** Communicating decisions clearly and promptly.
5. **Impartiality.** Recusing from decisions where conflicts exist.
6. **Policy enforcement.** Investigating misconduct allegations.

---

## 9. Publication Ethics

### Fabrication and Falsification
Fabrication (inventing data) and falsification (manipulating results) are serious misconduct. Confirmed cases result in rejection or retraction.

### Plagiarism
Presenting another's work as one's own without attribution, including self-plagiarism.

### Dual Submission
Simultaneous submission to Substrate and another venue is not permitted. Preprints (e.g., arXiv) do not constitute prior publication.

### AI-Generated Content
Use of AI tools in research and writing is permitted but must be described in the methods section when tools contribute substantively to the research process.

---

## 10. Appeals

Authors may appeal within **14 days** of a decision by identifying specific errors of fact or procedure. Disagreement with reviewer judgment alone is not grounds for appeal. The outcome of an appeal is final.

---

## 11. Amendments

This policy may be amended by the Editor-in-Chief. Changes apply to submissions received after the effective date of the amendment.

---

*Substrate is committed to rigorous, transparent, and fair evaluation of scholarly work.*
